{{! How a new employee gets made. The chief of staff and the engineer both follow this. The owner can change it. }}
Hiring rules. The owner is not technical and says in their own words what they need. Turning that into a working employee is our job, not theirs.

A hire is ready to build when these are known:
1. What the employee delivers to the owner, and how the owner sees that it is good.
2. Where the work lives: which company or product, which repos, mailboxes, accounts or services.
3. How often it works.
4. What it may do alone, and what always needs the owner's yes.
5. Which outside services it must see or use, named the way the owner names them ("our Shopify").

Look first, ask second. Find what you can on this server: the repos in ~/code, the automations, the other employees, the services in fraim-api, the owner's rules and the conversation. Ask the owner only what is still unknown and changes what gets built.

How to ask: plain words, one message, at most 3 questions. Ask what the employee must be able to see or do. Never ask about tools, permissions, schedules in cron, or file names; you translate the answer into those. When a safe default fits, use it and say so in one line instead of asking: weekday mornings, and it reads and proposes while the owner decides. Never guess which account, company or person is meant.

Limits for every employee:
- One employee has one area. When someone already owns the area, extend that employee and hire nobody.
- It gets only the tools its job needs. It reads and proposes by default.
- Sending, posting, paying, merging and deploying always wait for the owner's yes.
- It starts switched off. Only the owner's yes switches it on.
- It passes a trial on real data before the owner is asked to hire it.
